# Literature update (2026-11-12): where FORESIGHT stands after A, the encounters and the v2.3 guard

A second, targeted pass with Athena (idea checks, Ask a map, two literature reviews, paper search) on the questions the
results since 2026-11-02 raise. The first review is [`README.md`](README.md) / [`positioning.md`](positioning.md).
Athena feedback: `/ml/Athena/docs/feedback/2026-11-12-foresight-followup.md`.

## What was asked

| Question (from our results) | Athena tool | Answer in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Is the v2.3 camera-only mirror guard (VLM box → wedge on the map, slow check only within 1.5 m) new? | idea check | Yes in its sensor setting; closest work is RGB-D/ToF mirror segmentation (PDNet, SAMirror), reflected-object rejection with depth (Park & Park 2020), Mirror3D depth refinement. |
| Is "error type, not magnitude" (deliverable A) new? | idea check | Yes; NavTrust/RobustNav corrupt sensors at fixed levels and report per corruption, nobody plots navigation error against depth error type. But see *Safe Depth* below, found by the literature review, not by the idea check. |
| Latency-honest evaluation + encounter episodes? | idea check | No direct counterpart; closest are evaluation papers (Anderson 2018, Kadian 2019 sim2real) and NavTrust. |
| Can a dedicated mirror segmenter replace the VLM yes/no? | Ask a map (mirror) | RGB-only segmenters run ~30 fps (GDNet-B-M 0.034 s/image; HetNet reports FPS/params), but fail when reflection and surroundings look alike. A real, cheaper baseline for B. |
| How do depth foundation models behave on mirrors? | Ask a map (depth) | They read mirrors as cavities (UniDepthV2) or are "vulnerable to reflections" (DA-V2, large mirrors); fixes exist (inpaint-then-pseudo-label fine-tuning, Costanzino et al. 2023; Marigold handles reflections better). |
| Robust occupancy from noisy depth? | Ask a map (robustness), literature review | The robustness map has nothing on mapping; the review found uncertainty-aware "safe depth" (below). |
| When should a slow module be invoked? | Ask a map (dual-system) | Mostly fixed-rate; one 2026 paper formalises triggering as a decision problem and says published triggers lack measured costs. |
| Geometric (depth-gap) vs appearance mirror cues? | Ask a map (mirror) | Depth discontinuity is a strong cue but RGB-D methods are "misled by doorways and general depth changes" (Symmetry-Aware Transformer, 2022): the same false positives our gap candidates made (6/26 on real mirrors). |
| Do VLMs tell reflections from real objects? | paper search | *When Depth Lies* / MIRA-Bench (2026): no, and adding depth makes them worse. |

## The three papers that change the positioning

### 1. *From Depth to Safe Depth* (Firouzi Daghigh & Ayat, arXiv 2611.13769, 10 Nov 2026)

Closest work to deliverable A, two days old. Gazebo + ROS 2 Nav2, procedural corridor/hall worlds with a glass
partition, blank walls and a dark region; 120 paired episodes; exact McNemar tests (the same protocol shape as ours).

| | Success | Collision |
|---|---|---|
| GT depth | 93% | 2% |
| Depth Anything V2 metric (ViT-S, Hypersim), raw | 37% | 63% |
| + constant margin (depth × 0.48) | 30% | 5% (65% stuck/timeout) |
| + learned log-depth correction µ (0.58 M head, trained on sim) | 60% | 15% |
| + µ − kσ, conformal k = 3 (theirs) | 70% | 3% |
| Glass episodes (16): GT / raw / theirs | 94% / 0% / 37% | |

Their diagnosis matches ours: DA-V2 over-estimates near range (59.8% of pixels < 3 m by > 0.2 m), and "the ratio of
predicted to true depth varied between roughly 0.8 and 2 across scenes", so they reject a constant scale. What they
do not try is **per-scene online scale calibration**, which is what our FloorScale does (no training, from the
known camera height and the floor). On HM3D (set A, 120 episodes) the map navigator with DA-V2 succeeds 79% vs 83% with
GT depth (n.s.); without FloorScale 35% with 34% crashes. So:

- **A gains a direct comparison**: training-free per-scene scale calibration recovers most of the GT gap on real
  scanned homes, where they need a trained head plus conformal uncertainty in procedural worlds. Different simulator
  and planner, so this is a contrast, not a head-to-head.
- Their glass result (raw 0%, best 37% vs GT 94%) is the same failure as our mirror encounters (DA 64% / 25%
  crashes, GT 82%), and they note their simulated depth camera sees glass as solid, as Habitat's does.
- Their "where the margin goes matters" (σ spends caution only where errors are) is H-A's argument from the other
  side: errors are structured, so uniform fixes waste caution.
- On their own depth metric (near over-estimation: readings < 3 m over-estimated by > 0.2 m), our set-A traces give
  98% for raw DA-V2 and 11% after FloorScale (their trained head: 59.8% → 12.0%). Forward ray, not per pixel. Added to
  `docs/results/depth_error_A.md`.

### 2. *When Depth Lies* / MIRA-Bench (Yin et al., arXiv 2610.41651, Oct 2026)

VLMs misclassify reflected vs directly seen objects and depth input makes them worse. This explains what the v2.2
wall log showed: neither the Qwen box confidence nor the Laya confidence separated real mirrors from false ones
(precision flat at 41–60% across thresholds). It argues for using the VLM only as a coarse "is there a mirror,
where" proposer and letting the map's geometry (wedge on the route) decide, which is what v2.3 does.

### 3. *When to Ask for Help* (Zhang, 2026): slow-module scheduling in dual-system VLAs

Formalises when to invoke the slow module as a decision with measured costs and says published triggers lack them.
FORESIGHT's guard measures exactly these costs (slow checks per episode, time lost, wall precision, crashes avoided),
so B can report the trigger as a measured decision, not only as an architecture. Cite in B.

## Mirrors and glass in robot navigation: still a LiDAR literature

Athena's review "Handling mirrors and glass in robot navigation and mapping with cameras" (15 sources, from abstracts;
saved in Athena as review `4423ea70`) found that the navigation-side work is almost entirely LiDAR: intensity-based
mirror detection that edits the occupancy grid (Mora et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2024, > 97% accuracy), LiDAR glass
detection from range variation (Tibebu et al. 2021), reflection planes in plane SLAM (Li, Zhao & Schwertfeger 2024),
ultrasonic + laser fusion (Wei et al. 2018), GlassGuard (2026), the 3DRef RGB+LiDAR benchmark (2024). Camera-side,
only a SLAM-accuracy study with an ML mirror detector (Aydin et al. 2025) and onboard aerial glass detection with
sensor fusion (Hopkins et al., ICRA 2025). Writing a camera-detected mirror into a self-built occupancy map, on a
robot with no range sensor, remains uncovered; FORESIGHT's guard sits in that slot. The LiDAR line also gives the
right vocabulary for the write-up: "occupancy grid map modification" and "phantom obstacles / free-space
contamination behind reflective surfaces".
